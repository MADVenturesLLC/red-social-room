## Summary (5 lines)

1. Cost: every paid call gets one ledger row with a reservation before it is sent. xAI's actual cost comes from `usage.cost_in_usd_ticks`. Google's is computed from token counts at published rates and labeled "estimated". Edits on xAI are reserved and reconciled at the observed $0.07. A call that may have been sent but has no cost answer keeps its reservation until it is reconciled.
2. Results: a 200 response with no image is a refusal, shown plainly with the provider's text, and its cost is still recorded. Errors are mapped by the response body, not only the HTTP status (Google's bad key came back as 400 `API_KEY_INVALID`).
3. Safety rails: a free key check runs before the first paid call; 429 retries are bounded (at most 2 automatic retries, never a wait over 30 s); 5xx and timeouts are never retried automatically; the cap month is the calendar month in America/New_York.
4. Storage and sizing: raw provider responses are never stored or logged; only the image, the text, and the metadata we need are kept. Every export goes through a final exact-size crop, because Google's ratios are approximate and xAI has no 4:5.
5. Michael decided four product questions at 10:32 PM ET (block real-person prompts before sending, Grok 3:4 then crop to 4:5, widen with Nano Banana 2 first with a local or Recraft fallback, Google `store=false` plus deleting the spike's stored calls, which is done). This amendment implements those choices. Five narrower questions are still **Pending Founder decision**: how the real-person check detects names and fails, which outpaint fallback exists on the iMac, xAI zero data retention, output resolution, and xAI quality. Two more questions are pending Michael's decision in the PR Issues room: item 16's narrower spike rule, and whether spike runs that stop early exit non-zero (section 2.2, P7 and P8).
